## Why I chose it this way

- **Training moved from Colab to the Mac.** The merged dataset, `n640_v1/best.pt` and a working
  torch-with-MPS venv are all already local. Colab's T4 is ~3× faster per epoch (122 s measured from
  v1's own `results.csv`, against ~6 min here), but free Colab disconnects after 3–4 h and needs me
  sitting with it. Locally the queue runs unattended overnight. I traded wall-clock for not having
  to babysit.
- **v2 continues from v1's weights rather than restarting.** v1's val mAP50 was still climbing at
  epoch 50 (0.6788 → 0.6876 over the last six) with both val losses still falling. Still improving
  and not overfitting means more training is the cheapest accuracy available.
- **Every ablation arm shares one budget, one batch size, one seed.** Twelve epochs each, not fifty,
  because four 50-epoch arms is 20 h. The arms exist to rank a factor against a control, so what
  matters is that they are identical apart from the one change — not that they reach v1's score.
- **No class was removed.** The plan's rule was to drop anything under 0.15 AP50 with under 150
  training instances. The weakest class is bicycle at 0.453. The rule simply did not fire.
- **The data collection v2 was supposed to include is deferred, and I said so in writing.** Distant
  stairs, chair→person and clutter→dog hard negatives all need photographs that do not exist in any
  of my three source datasets. No amount of retraining invents them. v2 is "same data, trained
  longer", written down that way rather than dressed up.

## What broke and how I fixed it

- **yolo11s does not fit.** Nano at batch 32 / 640 px already uses 9.5 GB, and this machine has
  16 GB of unified memory. Rather than run one arm at a smaller batch and confound it, I dropped
  *every* arm to batch 16, with an automatic retry at batch 8 if the small model still runs out.
- **`close_mosaic` would have silently killed the augmentation ablation.** Its default is 10 epochs,
  meaning "turn mosaic off for the last 10". On a 12-epoch run that is 10 of 12 epochs with mosaic
  already off — so the "augmentation on" control would have been almost unaugmented and the arm
  would have shown nothing. Scaled it to 20 % of the budget, which is what v1 used (10 of 50).
- **MPS gives no dataloader workers.** Ultralytics prints "Using 0 dataloader workers" on MPS, so
  loading happens on the main process. Part of why an epoch is 6 min here and 2 min on a T4.

## What I learned (in my own words)

- **An under-trained model and an overfitting model look different in the loss curves, and it is
  worth knowing which you have.** Overfitting = training loss falling while validation loss turns
  up. v1 had both still falling, which is a different problem with a much cheaper fix.
- **Read the confusion matrix by column, not just along the diagonal.** The background column says
  the model's failure is *missing things* (curb 0.57 missed, pole 0.50, crosswalk 0.45), while the
  largest genuine class-vs-class mix-up in the whole matrix is 0.12. That points v2 at recall and
  rules out re-labelling work I might otherwise have done.
- **"int8 is faster" is not a fact about quantisation, it is a fact about a kernel library on a
  particular chip.** Mine came out 3.5× smaller and ~30 % *slower*, because dynamic quantisation
  converts activations at every layer boundary and the fp32 kernels it was competing with are
  already well optimised here.
- **A default can invalidate an experiment without failing.** `close_mosaic=10` would have produced
  a perfectly clean-looking table of numbers that meant nothing.
